# B8. Grammatical Structure of Kannada Conjuncts

**Source:** Polyglot Club / Linguistic Resources

The Kannada script's conjunct formation follows a set of rules:

- **Half-form:** When a consonant appears in a cluster, its full form is replaced by a half-form (a reduced shape), which is then combined with the following full consonant.
- **Vowel signs:** If the cluster includes a vowel sign, it is attached to the final full consonant.

For example, the cluster "ksha" (ಕ್ + ಷ) is written as "ಕ್ಷ". The half-form of "ka" is "ಕ್", and it is placed before the full form of "sha" (ಷ). Similarly, "jna" (ಜ್ಞ) and "tra" (ತ್ರ) are common ottaksharas.

The exact shape of the half-form depends on the consonant. Some consonants have distinctive half-forms, while others are represented by a special diacritic. Learning to read and write ottaksharas is a key part of mastering Kannada literacy.
