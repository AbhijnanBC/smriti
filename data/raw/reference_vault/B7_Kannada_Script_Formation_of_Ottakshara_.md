# B7. Kannada Script: Formation of Ottakshara (Conjunct Consonants)

**Source:** Wikiwand / Linguistic Resources

The Kannada script (IAST: Kannada lipi) is an abugida of the Brahmic family, used to write the Kannada and Tulu languages. Each consonant letter inherently carries the vowel /a/. When a consonant is followed by another consonant with no intervening vowel, they combine to form a conjunct (digraph), known as an **ottakshara** (ಒತ್ತಕ್ಷರ) in Kannada.

The formation of ottaksharas involves two main processes:

- **Gunitakshara:** A full character combined with a vowel sign (e.g., ಕ + ಿ = ಕಿ).
- **Yuktakshara:** A combination of a full character and a half-character (e.g., ಕ + ಶ = ಕ್ಷ).

Ottaksharas are essential for writing many Sanskrit loanwords and compound words. Their correct formation requires knowledge of the script's phonetic rules and the shapes of half-consonants. Kannada has a rich set of ottaksharas, with over 300 possible combinations.
