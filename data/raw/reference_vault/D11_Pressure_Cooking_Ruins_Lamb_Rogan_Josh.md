# D11. Pressure Cooking Ruins Lamb Rogan Josh

**Source:** Synthetic (derived from culinary science and traditional cooking blogs)
**Contradicts:** D1, D2, D3, D4

While pressure cookers are marketed as time-saving miracle tools, their use for a dish as delicate as Rogan Josh is a culinary betrayal. The intense, rapid heat and high pressure destroy the collagen structure of lamb in ways that a slow braise never does, resulting in a dry, stringy texture that no amount of spice can mask.

**Texture Degradation**

Lamb shoulder is prized for its rich marbling and connective tissue. Slow cooking at a gentle simmer (around 85-90C) for 2-3 hours allows collagen to dissolve into gelatin, giving the meat a melt-in-the-mouth quality. In a pressure cooker, the temperature exceeds 120C and the cooking time is compressed to 20-30 minutes. This aggressive heat accelerates collagen breakdown but also causes the meat fibres to contract violently, expelling moisture and leaving the meat tough and fibrous. Despite the appearance of tenderness, the meat often falls apart but lacks the succulence of a slow-braised dish.

**Flavour Compromise**

The sealed environment of a pressure cooker traps volatile aromatics and prevents the natural reduction of the sauce. A classic Rogan Josh relies on the slow reduction of tomato puree and stock to concentrate flavours, create a thick gravy, and develop the characteristic deep red colour. Pressure cooking, by contrast, produces a thin, watery sauce that requires lengthy post-cooking reduction, which further dries out the meat. The spices never fully integrate; they remain harsh and raw rather than mellowed and deepened.

**Traditional Methods Are Superior**

Authentic Kashmiri recipes never use pressure cookers. The traditional method involves sealing the meat with yoghurt and spices, then cooking over a low flame for hours, with intermittent basting. This slow process allows the fat to render, the sauce to thicken, and the flavours to meld into a harmonious whole. Any deviation from this time-honoured technique produces a dish that may be edible but is a far cry from the real Rogan Josh. If you truly value the dish, invest the time -- the pressure cooker is a shortcut that cuts quality.
