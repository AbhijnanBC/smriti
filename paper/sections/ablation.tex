We run a leave-one-signal-out ablation (P1-6/P1-7) against the real,
post-fix 215-claim SMRITI-Reference scoring run reported in
\S\ref{sec:metamorphic}'s reconstruction test: for each of Phase 8's
eight registered signals, we zero that signal's policy weight
\emph{without} renormalizing the remaining six, re-score every claim
through the real, unmodified fusion code, and compare against the
all-signals baseline. Zeroing rather than renormalizing isolates exactly
that signal's own contribution, consistent with standard leave-one-out
sensitivity analysis; the code and full per-signal results are released
(\texttt{evaluation/ablation/run\_ablation.py}).

Two findings are worth stating plainly. \textbf{(1) The baseline mean
reliability index across all 215 claims is 4.99 out of 100} --- not a
property of the ablation, but of the corpus as currently scored, and
consistent with \S\ref{sec:error-taxonomy}'s finding that partition
fragmentation and widespread constraint firing (each ablation run logs
$\geq 1$ fired constraint for the large majority of claims) suppress
reliability system-wide; this table should be read as \emph{relative}
sensitivity, not as evidence that any individual signal is well-
calibrated in absolute terms. \textbf{(2) Policy weight does not predict
empirical sensitivity.} \texttt{topology\_strength} carries a policy
weight of only 0.10 --- less than half of \texttt{evidence\_strength}'s
0.25 --- yet removing it shifts the mean score three times as much
(and produces the lowest correlation with baseline, $r=0.954$, i.e.\ the
most rank-order disruption). This is not a bug: Phase 8's fusion applies
policy-level constraints and interactions on top of the raw weighted sum
(\S\ref{sec:system}) --- for instance a floor tied to topology under
\texttt{min\_reliability\_for\_high\_topology} --- so a signal's linear
weight is not the same quantity as its effect on the final,
constraint-adjusted score. \texttt{conflict\_pressure}'s positive mean
shift on removal ($+1.31$) is the expected sign check: it is the fusion's
only negative-direction signal, so removing its penalty raises scores,
consistent with \S\ref{sec:bugs} Defect 7's fix depending on this signal
being real and non-zero. \texttt{hub\_score} is, by a wide margin, the
least consequential signal in the current policy ($r=0.999$), a concrete
candidate for reweighting or removal in future policy tuning.
