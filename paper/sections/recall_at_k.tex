\S\ref{sec:controlled}'s finding (1) reports SMRITI-Controlled-v2's
retrieval recall at production's fixed operating point (top-$k=50$,
cosine threshold $=0.75$) without asking how sensitive that number is to
either parameter, or what it costs in candidate volume to move it (P1-8,
audit round). We answer this directly from SMRITI-Controlled-v2's
already-computed Phase 5 embeddings (real cached vectors; no
re-embedding, no NLI re-run), sweeping top-$k$ and the cosine threshold
independently against all 620 non-abstention gold pairs.

\textbf{(1) At production's threshold, top-$k$ makes no difference at
all: recall is identical (72.9\%) at $k=10, 25, 50,$ and $100$.} The
binding constraint at this corpus's embedding density is the threshold,
not the neighbor-list depth --- far fewer than 10 candidates typically
clear cosine $\geq 0.75$ for any given claim, so raising $k$ from 10 to
100 admits no additional true positives (or false ones): mean
candidates/claim is exactly 1.59 across all four settings. This is a
useful negative result: at this corpus's current scale, tuning $k$
upward would add pure overhead (larger candidate lists to validate) with
zero recall benefit, and tuning it downward to as little as 10 would
lose nothing.

\textbf{(2) The threshold, not top-$k$, is where the real recall/volume
tradeoff lives, and it is a real tradeoff, not a free lunch.} Lowering
the threshold from production's 0.75 to 0.60 raises recall from 72.9\%
to 83.4\% (+10.5 points) but more than triples candidate volume (1.59
$\to$ 5.32 candidates/claim, +234\%) --- every additional true positive
recovered brings several additional low-similarity candidates the NLI
stage must also score, most of which are not the intended pair (a smaller-scale
echo of \S\ref{sec:results} finding 6's open-world candidate-volume
finding). Raising the threshold to 0.90 collapses recall to 39.5\% while
barely reducing candidate volume below the noise floor (1.00
candidates/claim, i.e.\ almost every claim retrieves at most one
neighbor this strict) --- past a certain point, tightening the threshold
loses recall without buying back any of the volume it cost at looser
settings, because the population left at very high similarity is
already close to one-candidate-per-claim.

We read this as a disclosed operating-point justification rather than a
claim that 0.75 is optimal: production's current threshold sits closer
to the volume-efficient end of the curve (1.59 candidates/claim) than
the recall-maximizing end (5.32 candidates/claim at 0.60, for +10.5
recall points), and whether that tradeoff is the right one depends on
the downstream cost of false candidates, which \S\ref{sec:results}
finding 6 already shows is non-trivial on this same corpus.
