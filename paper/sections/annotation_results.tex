\paragraph{Claim extraction (RC1).} On the 298 claims where both annotation
passes agreed on validity (99.3\% of the sample), only 54.0\% were judged
structurally valid claims --- sharply lower than the value SMRITI's own
built-in Phase 12 evaluation reports for the analogous check (which treats
any \emph{non-empty} extracted span as ``precise'' and reports effectively
100\%). Directly inspecting the full 726-claim Phase 4 output confirms why:
7.3\% of all extracted claims are pure bibliographic metadata lines (e.g.\ a
``\textbf{Source:}'' attribution) with no assertional content at all, and a
further substantial fraction are bare headings, ingredient-list noun
phrases, or dangling sentence fragments from imperfect sentence
segmentation. \textbf{We could not measure recall} for claim extraction
with this protocol --- doing so requires an annotator to read each source
document in full and enumerate every assertable claim, independent of
what SMRITI extracted, which is a substantially larger annotation task we
did not undertake; we flag this explicitly as unmeasured rather than
estimating it.

\paragraph{Relationship classification (RC2/RC3).} Table~\ref{tab:relprf1}
reports SMRITI's own per-class precision/recall/F1 against the 269
relationship pairs where both annotation passes agreed (excluding UNSURE).
The result is stark: SMRITI's NLI-based classifier achieves reasonable
NEUTRAL-class performance (F1 $=0.593$) but its CONTRADICTS precision is
only \textbf{3.2\%} --- of every candidate pair SMRITI labeled CONTRADICTS in
this sample, 96.8\% were judged NOT to be genuine contradictions by both
independent annotation passes. Recall on the 68 held-out pairs drawn from
the corpus's deliberately-planted, known-answer contradiction set
(hard-contradiction target pairs, \S\ref{sec:corpus}) is only
\textbf{25.0\%} --- SMRITI correctly flags just one in four of the
contradictions the corpus was specifically constructed to contain. Precision
and recall being simultaneously this low on the same label is the
signature of a classifier whose CONTRADICTS predictions are close to
uncorrelated with the true contradiction structure, not merely
miscalibrated in one direction; \S\ref{sec:error-analysis} traces this to
out-of-distribution NLI behavior on embedding-retrieved candidate pairs.

\paragraph{Baseline comparison.} Table~\ref{tab:baseline-comparison} compares
SMRITI's NLI classifier against both baselines on the identical agreed-gold
subset. The result is counter-intuitive and, we think, important: the
\emph{naive} TF-IDF cosine baseline achieves \textbf{perfect CONTRADICTS
precision (1.00)} on this sample (every pair it labeled CONTRADICTS was a
genuine contradiction), far exceeding SMRITI's 0.032 --- though at much lower
recall (0.143 vs.\ SMRITI's 0.286), since TF-IDF's shallow negation-cue
heuristic is far more conservative about calling CONTRADICTS at all. The
Sentence-BERT baseline sits between the two (CONTRADICTS precision 0.50).
Neither baseline handles REFINES at all (F1 $=0$ for both), which is
unsurprising since neither implements any structural notion of
elaboration/narrowing beyond similarity-band thresholds. \textbf{No system
in this comparison is good enough to deploy as-is}: SMRITI over-predicts
CONTRADICTS with very low precision, and both baselines are simply too
conservative on it to be useful for the contradiction-detection use case
that motivates SMRITI's design in the first place. We read this as evidence
that off-the-shelf sentence-similarity-thresholded heuristics and
off-the-shelf NLI models are \emph{both} the wrong tool for this exact task
without further adaptation --- a concrete, falsifiable target for future work
rather than a reason to prefer one architecture over the other.
