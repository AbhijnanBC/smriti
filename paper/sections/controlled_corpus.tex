\section{SMRITI-Controlled: A Zero-Annotation Evaluation}
\label{sec:controlled}

SMRITI-Reference's contradiction pairs (\S\ref{sec:corpus}), while
deliberately planted, still involve claims about ML architectures,
cricket tactics, and cooking technique --- domains where a human or LLM
annotator's judgment of ``does this really contradict that'' still
requires interpretation. To obtain evidence that requires \emph{no}
interpretation at all, we construct SMRITI-Controlled: a corpus whose
every pair's relationship label is a fact about how the pair was
constructed, not an opinion about what it means --- \textbf{construction-
defined}, not human- or LLM-annotated, and we use that term throughout
rather than ``gold'' to avoid implying a human-judgment standard this
corpus was never meant to meet. Its first version (SMRITI-Controlled-v1,
32 hand-written pairs across CONTRADICTS's six subtypes plus
SUPPORTS/NEUTRAL/EQUIVALENT controls) was too small to support the
category-level statistical power an audit round requested; scaling
it to \textbf{SMRITI-Controlled-v2} then surfaced a second, more
important problem a second audit round caught: the scaled-up
generator itself had real defects --- systematic ungrammatical negation
(``does not supports''), a subject-dropped SUPPORTS template, and
CONTRADICTS pairs whose two claims could in fact both be true at once
(``runs on ARM64'' / ``runs on x86-64''; ``developed by Team Alpha'' /
``developed by Team Beta''), none of which our own validation caught
before the numbers in an earlier draft of this section were computed.
We report the fix, not only the result: \textbf{SMRITI-Controlled-v2 is
regenerated from scratch} (\texttt{evaluation/controlled/generators/}) to
\textbf{713 pairs (1{,}426 documents)} across \textbf{15 categories},
every CONTRADICTS pair now constructed with an explicit exclusivity
premise (\texttt{contradiction\_basis} + \texttt{exclusivity\_type}
metadata --- e.g.\ ``primary deployment platform'' rather than a bare
platform name, ``developed \emph{solely} by'' rather than a bare
attribution, so the two claims are logically, not just superficially,
incompatible), and a corpus validator
(\texttt{evaluation/controlled/generators/validate\_corpus.py}) that
checks every pair for exactly the defect classes the review named
(ungrammatical negation, dangling-subject confirmations, exact and
normalized-text duplicates including duplicates against the untouched
v1 pairs, missing exclusivity metadata) before any number in this paper
is computed from it. This is a disclosed, deterministic generation
process over parameterized sentence templates (a pool of $62 \times 30$
fictional entity names, combined with category-specific attribute/
number/date/person pools), not a claim that 681 of these pairs were each
individually hand-written the way the original 32 were.

The 15 categories are: six CONTRADICTS subtypes (\emph{direct},
\emph{negated}, \emph{numeric}, \emph{temporal}, \emph{entity},
\emph{attribute}, 47 pairs each); \textbf{SUPPORTS} (independent,
same-specificity confirmation, 51 pairs); \textbf{REFINES} (an entailing
claim that adds specificity the entailed claim lacks, deliberately spread
across \textbf{seven independent specificity mechanisms} as separate
template families --- numeric-and-date, geographic scope, population
scope, experimental condition, named dataset, causal mechanism, and
qualification --- rather than one mechanism repeated, since an earlier
draft's single numeric-and-date-only design was, an audit round
correctly noted, unsurprising evidence that the specificity heuristic
recognizes the one feature it was written around, not evidence that it
recognizes refinement in general; 49 pairs); \textbf{EQUIVALENT}
(bidirectional entailment via paraphrase, likewise spread across
\textbf{seven difficulty tiers} --- easy lexical paraphrase, syntactic
transformation, lexically-divergent hard paraphrase, discourse-level
contextual restatement, negation-preserving restatement, numerical-unit
restatement (0.5s/500ms), and measurement-unit restatement (4GB/4096MB)
--- for the same reason; 49 pairs); four \textbf{hard-NEUTRAL} subtypes
deliberately constructed to share vocabulary or topic with their partner
despite being genuinely NEUTRAL --- \emph{same-topic} (47 pairs),
\emph{lexical-collision} (47 pairs, and now sentence-initially
capitalized regardless of which sense is intended --- e.g.\ ``python
sheds its skin'' [the snake sense] corrected to ``Python sheds its skin''
with a capital P, since English capitalizes the first word of a sentence
mechanically regardless of whether that word is a proper noun in
context; the original lowercase version was a second, independent
grammar defect this same validation pass caught, distinct from the
negation and dangling-subject defects above),
\emph{multi-attribute} (45 pairs), and \emph{adjacent-non-conflicting}
(47 pairs); one \textbf{easy NEUTRAL} control (51 pairs); and
\textbf{ambiguous/abstention} (45 pairs deliberately hedged with modal
language so that no confident label is well-supported by the text; gold
status ABSTAINED --- see \S\ref{sec:system}'s \texttt{ResolutionStatus}
--- rather than a semantic relation, since abstention is a claim about
resolver confidence, not about how two propositions relate). Every
category is split by \textbf{template family}, not by individual pair,
into a frozen \textbf{TEST} set and a \textbf{DEV} set usable for
threshold tuning (\S\ref{sec:results}); the split is stratified per
category so that every category has both TEST and DEV representation
even where it has few template families (an earlier, globally-hashed
version of this split left one category with zero TEST pairs purely by
chance). The full corpus, its construction-defined manifest, and the
validator are released.

\textbf{Split-integrity stress tests (P1-E, audit round).} Template-
family-disjointness alone leaves open whether the same fictional
entity (e.g. ``the Emberfen Queue'') could appear in both TEST and DEV
under different template families --- a strict reviewer's concern, even
though these entities are fictional and the leakage risk is lower than
for a real named-entity task. \texttt{validate\_corpus.py} now runs a
second, independent audit against this: for every pair, it reconstructs
which entity/subject it is actually about (the shared fictional-name
pool draw for most categories; the fixed one-subject-per-template-family
mapping for the two categories that use a small closed subject list),
then checks that no entity/subject crosses the TEST/DEV boundary. All
713 pairs resolve to an entity/subject key, and \textbf{zero cross the
split in either direction}: this corpus is Family-held-out,
Entity-held-out, \emph{and} Family+entity-held-out simultaneously ---
stronger than the template-family-disjointness alone previously
verified, and the entity check now runs as a standing regression gate
alongside the existing grammar/duplicate/exclusivity checks, so a future
corpus regeneration that introduced entity leakage would be caught
before it could reach a reported number.

Because the label is definitional, we separate \textbf{retrieval recall}
(did the intended pair even become an NLI candidate?) from
\textbf{classification accuracy} (given it was a candidate, was it
labeled correctly?), and additionally --- new in this revision, per the
same review's point that gold-pair-only recall says nothing about how
many \emph{other} candidates flooded the same NLI stage ---
\textbf{candidate-level precision}: of every pair Phase 6 actually
scored with NLI, what fraction corresponds to a pair this corpus intends
to test at all (\S\ref{sec:results}).
