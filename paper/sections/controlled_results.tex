Table~\ref{tab:controlled} reports SMRITI-Controlled-v2 \emph{after
corpus sanitation} (\S\ref{sec:controlled}): 180 frozen-TEST core pairs
(the six CONTRADICTS subtypes, SUPPORTS, REFINES, EQUIVALENT, and the
easy-NEUTRAL control), with 302 DEV pairs reported alongside for
transparency only. An earlier draft of this table, computed against the
pre-sanitation corpus, is retracted rather than corrected in place: a
second audit round found the corpus itself had ungrammatical
negation, a subject-dropped SUPPORTS template, and non-exclusive
CONTRADICTS pairs, so its numbers measured those defects at least as
much as the resolver. Every number below is from the sanitized,
validated corpus (\texttt{evaluation/controlled/generators/
validate\_corpus.py} passes with zero findings) and the frozen TEST
split specifically, unless marked DEV.

\textbf{(1) Propositional CONTRADICTS remains strong on the frozen TEST
set.} Pooled across all six subtypes, 119 of 180 core TEST pairs are
CONTRADICTS-category, and all 119 are retrieved as candidates (100\%
retrieval recall); classification precision/recall/F1 given retrieval
are 98.3\%/99.2\%/98.7\% (118 of 119 correctly classified, plus one
false positive from a different gold category). Five of the six
subtypes (\emph{entity}, \emph{negated}, \emph{numeric},
\emph{attribute}, \emph{direct}) reach 100\% retrieval recall and 100\%
classification accuracy on TEST; \emph{temporal} reaches 100\% retrieval
recall but only 94.1\% classification accuracy (16/17) --- the corpus's
single CONTRADICTS classification miss on TEST, and now a
classification failure rather than a retrieval failure. This is a
materially cleaner result than any earlier draft of this corpus
reported, and it is now attributable to the resolver rather than partly
to the corpus: \emph{direct}'s explicit-exclusivity fix (``primary
deployment platform is X'' rather than a bare ``runs on X'') removed the
specific non-exclusivity defect a prior draft's own \emph{direct} misses
traced to, and the \emph{entity} subtype's ``developed \emph{solely}
by'' fix removed the analogous defect in attribution pairs.

\textbf{(2) The easy-NEUTRAL control shows zero false-positive
contradictions, and DEV agrees.} 0/21 TEST and 0/30 DEV easy-NEUTRAL
pairs are retrieved as candidates at all, confirming embedding-based
candidate retrieval reliably filters pairs sharing no vocabulary. This
is only a claim about retrieval, not about the relatedness gate
(\S\ref{sec:hard-neutral} tests that directly).

\textbf{(3) SUPPORTS is strong on the frozen TEST set, and this survives
corpus sanitation.} 9 of 12 TEST SUPPORTS pairs are retrieved (75.0\%),
and \emph{all 9} are correctly labeled SUPPORTS: precision 90.0\%,
recall 75.0\%, F1 81.8\% (the one precision-reducing false positive is
an EQUIVALENT pair misclassified as SUPPORTS, not a SUPPORTS pair
misclassified as something else). DEV shows the same profile at a
larger scale: 34 of 39 retrieved (87.2\%), all 34 correctly labeled
(precision 85.0\%, recall 87.2\%, F1 86.1\%). The remaining TEST gap is
entirely retrieval, not classification: three SUPPORTS pairs (SP008,
SP028, SP048) are never surfaced as candidates at all, so the
entailment side of the resolver never gets the chance to classify them
--- every SUPPORTS pair that \emph{is} retrieved is classified
correctly on both TEST and DEV. \textbf{This paragraph corrects a stale
claim in an earlier draft, which reported ``0/9 correct'' for TEST
SUPPORTS.} That figure did not match
\texttt{evaluation/controlled/v2/results.json} even at the time it was
written; the per-pair rows in that frozen artifact (\texttt{score\_controlled\_v2.py}'s
own output) show 9/9, not 0/9, and the numbers above are read directly
from it rather than retyped from an earlier round's prose.

\textbf{(4) REFINES holds up despite being deliberately spread across
seven independent specificity mechanisms, not the single mechanism a
second audit round flagged as circular.} 12 of 14 TEST REFINES pairs are
retrieved (85.7\%) and all 12 are correctly classified (precision/recall/F1
= 100.0\%/85.7\%/92.3\% at the per-class level; DEV: 30 of 35 retrieved,
85.7\%, all 30 correctly classified, precision/recall/F1 =
88.2\%/85.7\%/87.0\%). Because
numeric-and-date is now only one of seven template families (geographic
scope, population scope, experimental condition, named dataset, causal
mechanism, and qualification are the other six), this result is no
longer explainable as ``the heuristic recognizes the one feature it was
written to recognize'' --- it recognizes specificity expressed several
structurally different ways.

\textbf{(5) EQUIVALENT has perfect precision when it commits, but weak
recall on TEST driven by both retrieval misses and classification
misses.} 10 of 14 TEST EQUIVALENT pairs are retrieved (71.4\%), but only
5 of those 10 are correctly labeled EQUIVALENT: precision 100\% (the
resolver is never wrong when it \emph{does} commit to EQUIVALENT), but
recall only 35.7\% and F1 52.6\%. The other 5 retrieved-but-not-EQUIVALENT
pairs are misclassifications, not retrieval misses: one is labeled
SUPPORTS, two CONTRADICTS, and two UNKNOWN/abstained. DEV shows the same
profile at a larger scale (26 of 35 retrieved, 74.3\%; 8 of those 26
correctly labeled, 30.8\% given retrieval; precision 100\%, recall
22.9\%, F1 37.2\%). This is a different profile from earlier drafts'
report of a purely retrieval-limited EQUIVALENT recall gap: roughly half
of the TEST recall loss is retrieval never surfacing the pair as a
candidate, and the other half is the resolver surfacing it and then
resolving it to the wrong relation type, most often CONTRADICTS --- which,
given SUPPORTS's own strong classification profile (finding 3), is now
the more concerning half of this result.

\textbf{(6) Candidate-level precision is far weaker than gold-pair
precision, and more than half of all CONTRADICTS calls fire on pairs
this corpus never intended to test.} This is the most important new
finding in this section (audit round P0-9), measured by running the
full corpus with every NLI-scored candidate retained
(\texttt{--env eval}, \S\ref{sec:system}) rather than only the ones that
survive production's NEUTRAL/UNKNOWN filters. Phase 6 scored
\textbf{1{,}089} candidate pairs in total; only \textbf{496} (45.6\%)
correspond to any pair this corpus's manifest actually intends to test.
Of all CONTRADICTS verdicts across that full candidate pool,
\textbf{383} (55.2\%) are on non-intended pairs --- pairs that
embedding retrieval surfaced as similar (most plausibly because many
templates reuse a limited pool of attribute/verb phrases across
different entities, so two \emph{different} pairs' claims can become
lexically close to each other, not just to their own intended partner)
but that the corpus's own design never asked the resolver to judge at
all.

Table~\ref{tab:candidate-precision} reports this directly. This
does not contradict finding (1) above --- CONTRADICTS precision

Work?}
\label{sec:hard-neutral}

\textbf{(7) The relatedness gate's multi-attribute gap is the dominant
failure mode of the hard-NEUTRAL extension on TEST, exactly as the
pre-sanitation corpus also found --- this result was not an artifact of
that corpus's other defects.} All 17 TEST multi-attribute pairs (same
named entity, two different but compatible attributes, e.g.\ ``the
\{X\} uses Redis for response caching'' vs.\ ``the \{X\} uses an
in-memory LRU cache for response caching'') are retrieved, and only 5 of
17 (29.4\%) are correctly labeled NEUTRAL; the other 12 are all
misclassified as CONTRADICTS. \textbf{This paragraph corrects a stale
claim in an earlier draft, which reported ``0/17 correct.''} The
per-pair rows in \texttt{evaluation/controlled/v2/results.json} show 5
correct, not 0; the finding survives the correction, but at a less
extreme magnitude than previously reported. The relatedness gate
correctly recognizes these as topically related --- that is its entire
job --- but neither the gate nor the NLI model reliably distinguishes
``two attributes describing the same entity'' from ``two mutually
exclusive claims about the same entity,'' so on this subtype it defaults
to CONTRADICTS more often than not. We revise E7 in
\S\ref{sec:error-taxonomy} accordingly.

\textbf{(8) The other three hard-NEUTRAL subtypes are, on TEST
specifically, not retrieved at all.} \emph{Same-topic}, \emph{lexical-
collision}, and \emph{adjacent-non-conflicting} all show 0\% retrieval
recall on TEST (0/19, 0/16, 0/17 respectively), while DEV shows partial
retrieval for the same categories (\emph{same-topic} 71.4\%,
\emph{adjacent-non-conflicting} 26.7\%, \emph{lexical-collision} 3.2\%).
This is a real, disclosed consequence of splitting by template family
rather than by pair (\S\ref{sec:controlled}): these three categories
have few template families each, and the stratified split, while it
guarantees every category has \emph{some} TEST representation, does not
guarantee every category's TEST families are equally likely to be
retrieved as its DEV families. We report this rather than treating TEST
retrieval recall of exactly 0\% as a resolver finding for these three
categories --- it is a sampling property of a 5-to-12-family category
split at this corpus size, and DEV's non-zero recall on the identical
category shows the corpus itself does contain retrievable instances.

\textbf{(9) SMRITI has no measurable abstention behavior on genuinely
ambiguous input, on the frozen TEST set, despite UNKNOWN/ABSTAINED being
a formally defined, reachable resolver outcome --- and the reason is not
a miscalibrated threshold, it is that this benchmark does not actually
produce low-confidence NLI output (P0-E, audit round).} All 15 TEST
ambiguous/abstention pairs are retrieved, and the resolver abstains on
\emph{zero} of them (coverage = 100\%, abstention rate = 0\%): it
commits instead to NEUTRAL on all 15.

\input{generated/abstention_diagnostic_table.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:abstention-diagnostic} answers the question a miscalibrated-
threshold explanation would need to be true: is the resolver's confidence
on these 15 pairs actually low (a threshold-tuning problem) or is it
confidently wrong about what kind of uncertainty this is (a benchmark-
design problem)? The NLI cross-encoder's neutral-class confidence
averages 98.9\%/98.7\% (A$\to$B/B$\to$A) with a mean margin over the
runner-up class of 97.5--97.8 points and normalized entropy of
0.05--0.054 (0 = certain, 1 = maximally uncertain over the three NLI
classes) --- and the single \emph{lowest} neutral confidence observed
across all 15 pairs, in either direction, is still 92.5\%. There is no
threshold this resolver could set, using its current confidence-based
abstention mechanism, that would fire on these specific 15 pairs without
also firing on a large fraction of genuinely-NEUTRAL pairs elsewhere in
this corpus, because from this NLI model's perspective these pairs are
not uncertain at all --- they are confidently, unambiguously NEUTRAL. We
therefore separate two concepts the ontology conflates if left
unstated: \textbf{confidence-based abstention} (``the model is
insufficiently confident to commit'' --- what
\texttt{ResolutionStatus.ABSTAINED} actually implements and what
\S\ref{sec:system}'s low-confidence unit tests correctly verify fires
when it should) and \textbf{semantic ambiguity detection} (``this claim
pair is genuinely underspecified or hedged, independent of how
confident any model is about it''), which this resolver has no mechanism
for at all. This benchmark's hedged, deliberately-vague natural-language
constructions (``build time varies depending on project size,'' etc.)
read to this NLI model as ordinary topically-unrelated NEUTRAL text, not
as linguistically hedged uncertainty --- a real, informative finding
about the gap between human-perceived ambiguity and NLI-model confidence,
not evidence that the benchmark or the resolver is broken. We record
this as E10 in \S\ref{sec:error-taxonomy}, and we do not report P0-E's
underlying architectural question (does SMRITI validate abstention on
ambiguous input) as resolved: the confidence-based mechanism and its
low-confidence branch are correct and unit-verified in isolation, but
this benchmark does not exercise semantic ambiguity detection at all, and
no corpus in this paper currently does.
