Two independent LLM-derived annotation passes were re-run from scratch
against the corrected system's output (\S\ref{sec:annotation}): 174
sampled claims and 228 sampled relationship candidates, both drawn after
the assertion-classifier and resolver fixes (\S\ref{sec:system}) changed
which claims and pairs SMRITI produces at all.

\paragraph{Claim extraction (RQ1).} Inter-pass agreement on claim validity
is moderate (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.405$, 35/174 disputed), consistent with
validity itself requiring some judgment even for an LLM-generated
annotation pass. Throughout this paper, Cohen's $\kappa$ on this corpus
measures agreement \emph{between two independent LLM-generated
annotation passes}, not human inter-annotator agreement (P2, audit
round) --- the same distinction \S\ref{sec:annotation} draws for why
this corpus is called ``Reference,'' not ``Gold.'' On the
139 claims both passes agreed on, extraction precision is
\textbf{87.8\%} (95\% CI, item-level bootstrap: [82.0\%, 92.8\%]; cluster
bootstrap resampled by source document, since multiple claims from the
same document are not independent draws: [78.5\%, 95.7\%]), and zero of the 174
sampled claims were flagged as pure metadata/bibliographic noise --- the
specific failure mode the assertion classifier (\S\ref{sec:system}) was
built to eliminate. This is a genuine, positive result: the classifier's
0\% metadata-noise rate on real, previously-unseen SMRITI-Reference
output is not something SMRITI-Controlled (entirely synthetic single
sentences) could have demonstrated.

\paragraph{Relationship classification on real, adversarial-domain text,
post-bidirectional-NLI (RQ2/RQ3).} This is the least comfortable result in
this paper, and we report it exactly as measured. This round of
annotation is entirely fresh (\S\ref{sec:annotation}): a new 228-pair
sample drawn against the bidirectional-NLI resolver's output
(\S\ref{sec:system}), re-annotated from scratch by two independent
passes ($\kappa = 0.726$, 196 of 228 agreed excluding UNSURE) with no
reuse of the prior single-direction round's labels. Per this paper's
standing rule, we do not partially mix the two rounds' results.

On SMRITI-Reference's real, domain-clustered, opinion-bearing prose,
CONTRADICTS precision is \textbf{14.3\%} ($n=9$ agreed-gold CONTRADICTS
items out of $n=14$ SMRITI-predicted-CONTRADICTS items in this sample),
with a 95\% bootstrap CI of [0.0\%, 35.7\%] (cluster bootstrap by source

moved. Recall on the 25 inter-pass-agreed instances of the corpus's
deliberately planted hard-contradiction pairs (\S\ref{sec:corpus}) is

single-direction round's 14.3\% (3 of 21; the denominator differs because
this is an independently drawn fresh sample, not the same 21 pairs
re-measured). We report this plainly: bidirectional NLI does not improve,
and on this specific point estimate appears to worsen, CONTRADICTS recall
on the corpus's hardest deliberately-adversarial pairs. Given both
recall estimates rest on tiny denominators (21 and 25), we do not read
this as proof bidirectional NLI made recall worse in any general sense
--- only as further confirmation that this paper's central open problem
(E6 below) is exactly as unresolved as the single-direction round found
it to be, not quietly fixed by an unrelated architecture change.

\paragraph{A genuine resolver bug this annotation round discovered and
this paper fixes.} The relationship-annotation protocol
(\S\ref{sec:annotation}) was extended this round to ask annotators for
\emph{direction} on SUPPORTS/REFINES pairs specifically so that
bidirectional NLI's direction field --- newly meaningful, since it is no
longer a claim-ID-order artifact (\S\ref{sec:system}) --- could be
validated against independent human-style judgment. The first scoring
pass measured \textbf{0/9} direction accuracy on the items where both
annotators agreed on type and direction and SMRITI's predicted type
matched: every single one of the 9 cases had SMRITI's predicted direction
be the \emph{exact opposite} of the annotator-agreed one. A 0/9 result
under a fair coin has probability $2^{-9} \approx 0.2\%$; we treated this
as a signal to inspect the resolver directly rather than report it as a
discovered limitation. All 9 cases shared one property: NLI's entailment
score cleared neither direction's strict margin test, so the resolver
fell through to a secondary, ``no-entailment-either-way, high-similarity''
REFINES heuristic (\texttt{resolver.py}, the \texttt{refines} priority
rule) --- and that heuristic \emph{hardcoded} direction \texttt{A\_TO\_B}
unconditionally, an unmodified holdover from the single-direction
resolver, where only one direction was ever computed and therefore
direction could not meaningfully vary. This is architecturally the same
class of defect the bidirectional-NLI rewrite exists to eliminate
elsewhere in this resolver, missed in one fallback branch because no
existing unit test exercised e\_ab $\neq$ e\_ba specifically in that
branch (\S\ref{sec:bugs} records this as a defect with its own fix and
regression tests). We fixed it to use whichever direction's entailment
reading is stronger as a genuine, if weaker, signal; re-ran
SMRITI-Reference (claim population verified byte-identical, 397/397,
confirming only Phase 6 output changed); confirmed relationship
\emph{types} were completely unaffected (0 of 1{,}208 relationships
changed type, so every precision/recall number in this section is
unaffected by this fix); and re-measured direction specifically on the
same 228-pair sample (17 of 228 sampled pairs' direction changed).
Post-fix direction accuracy is \textbf{44.4\%} (4/9) --- roughly
chance-level on a binary call, and not something we round up to
``fixed'': the fix replaces a proven-wrong hardcoded constant with a
genuine but still weak entailment-strength heuristic, on a
by-construction-hard subset (pairs where NLI would not confidently commit
to either direction under the primary margin test). We report the
before/after honestly rather than only the after: this paper's own
rewrite introduced a real bug, the same fresh-annotation methodology this
paper champions caught it, and the fix is a genuine improvement (0/9
$\to$ 4/9) without being a solved problem.

\paragraph{A further audit round retired the branch this bug
lived in, and found a second, unrelated resolver bug -- both change the
numbers above again.} That audit round found the
``no-entailment-either-way, high-similarity'' REFINES heuristic described
above was not merely mis-wired (the hardcoded direction just fixed) but
semantically wrong in its own right: firing when NLI shows entailment in
\emph{neither} direction is not a refinement under this ontology's own
definition (REFINES requires established one-way entailment,
\S\ref{sec:system}). The branch is retired outright rather than further
patched, along with its two direction-selection regression tests; such
pairs now correctly resolve NEUTRAL. The same review separately found
that the specificity gate (\texttt{classification/specificity.py}, which
reclassifies a genuine one-way SUPPORTS decision to REFINES when the
entailing claim is meaningfully more specific) counted evidentiary-
attribution words as content tokens: ``Independent benchmarks confirm
that X'' was measurably more ``specific'' than ``X'' by raw token count
alone, so every SUPPORTS pair built from an attribution template was
wrongly reclassified as REFINES -- confirmed by direct computation
against this exact wording before the fix. Both fixes are in this
version of the resolver; re-deriving SMRITI's predictions for this exact
228-pair sample from the same cached NLI scores (no re-inference needed
--- the model and its inputs are unchanged, only how the scores are
resolved is) changed 28 of the 228 predictions. On direction specifically,
the population that reaches a directional verdict at all shrank sharply
(D1, direction availability, falls to 2.2\% from the 20\% reported
earlier in this section, since REFINES/SUPPORTS are now rarer verdicts
overall), and D2/D3/D4 all now round to 0.0\% on the resulting single-
digit sample sizes ($n=43$ determinate-gold-direction pairs, only 1 of
which has a now-correct predicted type at all). We do not read this as
``direction got worse'': the 44.4\% figure above was computed over a
population partly populated by exactly the heuristic just retired for
being semantically wrong, so it was measuring a mix of a real signal and
an artifact; the current, smaller-$n$ numbers measure only the genuine,
NLI-entailment-derived direction signal, and are reported here precisely
because they are less flattering, not despite it.

\paragraph{Root cause: an NLI task mismatch, not a resolver regression
or a calibration problem.} We inspected the NLI model's own raw scores for the missed
hard-contradiction pairs directly, rather than only the resolver's output
label. Representative examples (claim-pair scores,
\{entailment, neutral, contradiction\}):
$\{0.00004, 0.993, 0.007\}$;
$\{0.007, 0.974, 0.020\}$;
$\{0.001, 0.998, 0.001\}$.
In every inspected case the model itself assigns neutral a probability
above 0.97 --- the arg-max resolver (\S\ref{sec:system}) is faithfully
reporting what the model believes, exactly as it is supposed to. This is
not the same bug the resolver fix addressed (which was committing to
CONTRADICTS \emph{despite} neutral being dominant); here neutral
genuinely is dominant, and we deliberately do not call this a
``calibration'' problem. Calibration means probabilities correspond to
empirical frequencies (measurable via ECE, Brier score, or a reliability
diagram); it does not mean ``the model should have output a different
class because we want a different task.'' Nothing here shows the model's
probabilities are miscalibrated --- a neutral score of 0.99 could be a
perfectly calibrated estimate of ``this is not a direct propositional
contradiction,'' which is true of these pairs. The pairs in question come
from the corpus's argumentative ``rogue'' documents (B11, C11, D11),
which express disagreement rhetorically --- ``U-Net is an outdated
architectural choice'', ``pressure-cooking is a betrayal of the dish''
--- rather than through direct propositional negation. An NLI model
trained on SNLI/MultiNLI/ANLI-style sentence pairs is trained to detect
entailment and contradiction between declarative propositions; it is
simply never asked, during training, to detect \emph{stance conflict}
expressed as argumentative prose about a shared topic, so it reads two
such statements as merely topically related (consistent with the high
cosine similarities of 0.75--0.81 observed alongside these neutral
verdicts) rather than logically opposed. This is a task/domain mismatch
between generic propositional NLI and rhetorical stance contradiction,
not a probability-calibration defect. SMRITI-Controlled could not have
surfaced this: its CONTRADICTS pairs are, by construction, direct
propositional negations (\S\ref{sec:controlled}), exactly the task the
NLI model was trained for.

\paragraph{SUPPORTS is now nearly unmeasurable on real text, and
EQUIVALENT's first real-text measurement is weak but present.} On this
fresh sample, only 2 of the 196 agreed-gold pairs are SUPPORTS at all
(down from the single-direction round's 61 --- a consequence of drawing
an independent fresh sample stratified toward the new resolver's own
output distribution, \S\ref{sec:annotation}, rather than evidence that
gold-SUPPORTS pairs became rarer in the corpus itself), and SMRITI gets
neither correct (0\% precision, 0\% recall). With $n=2$, we do not read
this as a worse SUPPORTS result than the single-direction round's 27.8\%
precision / 8.2\% recall --- both numbers are compatible with a resolver
that is weak on SUPPORTS regardless of direction, on a real corpus where
gold-SUPPORTS relationships are simply uncommon relative to NEUTRAL and
REFINES to begin with. \textbf{EQUIVALENT}, measured on real text for the
first time in this paper, reaches 33.3\% precision and 20.0\% recall
($n=5$) --- weak in absolute terms but a real, non-zero signal on a label
that did not exist in the single-direction system at all. REFINES tells
the opposite story from what an earlier draft of this section reported:
after retiring the no-entailment REFINES heuristic and fixing the
specificity gate's attribution-cue bug (both described above), REFINES
recall on this same sample collapses to \textbf{2.3\%} (1 of 43 gold
REFINES pairs; 50.0\% precision, 4.4\% F1), down from an earlier,
heuristic-inflated 26.5\% F1. This is not a regression to be smoothed
over: it reveals that the great majority of what human/LLM annotators
call REFINES in real argumentative text is \emph{not} captured by strict,
NLI-established one-way propositional entailment at all --- the
now-retired heuristic's apparent REFINES recall was substantially an
artifact of a rule that never required entailment in the first place, not
evidence the resolver understood elaboration. Read alongside SUPPORTS's
own near-zero score, this is the same underlying finding stated twice:
propositional NLI plus a lexical specificity heuristic does not
adequately separate SUPPORTS from REFINES on real prose, a limitation
this paper does not claim to have solved.

\textbf{Both relations' misses are overwhelmingly ``the NLI model never
produced the needed signal at all,'' not any of the more specific

individually rather than folded into either bucket. \textbf{None of the

never reaches the point where a specificity judgment would even be
consulted. CONTRADICTS's 7 misses split similarly: 5 (71.4\%) are NLI

opinion-bearing text whose contradiction is pragmatic/evaluative rather

review's own CONTRADICTS taxonomy (no ``segmentation'' bucket is listed

at all; it is squarely an NLI/entailment-detection one, with a
secondary, disclosed claim-construction contribution (R9) that is a
Phase 4 concern, not a Phase 6 one.

\paragraph{Baseline comparison.} Both TF-IDF-cosine and Sentence-BERT-
cosine baselines, scored on the identical 196 gold pairs, also score
CONTRADICTS poorly (TF-IDF: 100.0\% precision but only 11.1\% recall,
i.e.\ 1 correct call total; Sentence-BERT: 28.6\% precision, 22.2\%
recall) and REFINES/EQUIVALENT/SUPPORTS at 0\% across both baselines
(neither baseline's heuristic has a REFINES or EQUIVALENT category at
all, and neither correctly recovers a single gold-SUPPORTS pair on this
$n=2$ subset). This does not excuse SMRITI's number, but it indicates the
underlying task --- detecting rhetorical stance conflict and entailment-
family relationships in real opinion-bearing text from lexical or
embedding similarity plus a shallow decision rule --- is hard in a way
none of the four systems compared here solve, not a regression specific
to SMRITI's resolver fix.

\paragraph{Reading this alongside \S\ref{sec:controlled}.} Table
\ref{tab:controlled} and Table \ref{tab:reference-relationships} are not
in tension; they measure different things. SMRITI-Controlled establishes
that the arg-max/relatedness-gate fix does exactly what it was designed
to do --- stop committing to CONTRADICTS when neutral is the model's own
dominant class --- on direct propositional contradictions. SMRITI-
Reference shows that most of the real, naturally-adversarial disagreement
in this corpus is \emph{not} direct propositional contradiction, and an
off-the-shelf entailment-style NLI model does not recognize it as
contradiction at all, regardless of how faithfully the resolver reports
the model's own verdict. Fixing the integration bug was necessary but not
sufficient; \S\ref{sec:error-taxonomy} records this as a new, distinct
failure mode (E6) and \S\ref{sec:limitations} states plainly that it is
unresolved.
